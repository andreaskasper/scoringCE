## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Purpose of this Manual

This manual provides comprehensive guidance for judges using the tablet-based judging system at dance competitions. Whether you're a first-time judge or an experienced professional, this guide will help you understand all features and functions of the judging tablet system, ensuring accurate, fair, and efficient scoring.

The system is designed to streamline the judging process while maintaining the integrity and standards required by the World Swing Dance Council (WSDC) and other governing bodies.

### 1.2 Overview of the Judging Tablet System

The judging tablet system represents a modern, digital approach to dance competition scoring. Key features include:

- **Real-time scoring**: Your scores are continuously synchronized with the central scoring system
- **Multiple judging modes**: Choose the scoring method that works best for you
- **Automatic calculations**: The system handles all mathematical computations
- **Visual aids**: Color coding, markers, and notes help you track competitors
- **WSDC compliance**: Built-in adherence to WSDC registry event rules
- **Offline capability**: Continue judging even if internet connection is temporarily lost

The system supports both preliminary rounds (using the callback system) and finals (using placement scoring), accommodating various competition formats including Jack & Jill, Strictly Swing, Classic, Showcase, and Rising Star divisions.

### 1.3 WSDC Compliance and Standards

For WSDC Registry Events, this judging system is designed to comply with all current WSDC Registry Event Rules, including:

- **Callback System**: Standardized point values (Yes = 10, Alt1 = 4.5, Alt2 = 4.3, Alt3 = 4.2, No = 0)
- **Relative Placement**: Standard placement scoring for finals
- **Raw Scoring**: Chief Judges must assign unique numerical raw scores to all competitors
- **Minimum Requirements**: Support for required numbers of judges and finalists
- **Integrity Standards**: Prevention of tampering and unauthorized score changes

The system maintains audit trails and provides the reporting necessary for WSDC points registry submissions.

## 2. Getting Started

### 2.1 Receiving Your Tablet

**Before the Competition:**
- Tablets are distributed at the **Judge Meeting Point** before each round
- If there is a Competition Coordinator or if the Head Judge requests it, they will personally distribute and collect tablets
- Handle tablets with care - these are sensitive electronic devices
- **CRITICAL**: Keep all liquids and beverages away from tablets to prevent damage

**Tablet Care Guidelines:**
1. Hold the tablet securely with both hands when moving
2. Do not place tablets on surfaces where they could fall
3. Never place drinks, water bottles, or any liquids near tablets
4. Avoid placing heavy objects on top of tablets
5. Report any damage immediately to the Head Judge or Competition Coordinator
6. Keep tablets in a safe location when not actively judging

**After Each Round:**
- Return tablets to the Judge Meeting Point immediately after completing your scoring
- If a Competition Coordinator is assigned, return directly to them
- Do not leave tablets unattended

### 2.2 Initial Login

When you first receive your tablet and open the judging application, you'll see a list of available competitions.

**Step-by-Step Login Process:**

1. **Select Your Competition**
   - You'll see a list of currently running competitions
   - Tap the button for the competition you're assigned to judge
   - Examples: "Demo Jack&Jill final", "Demo Jack&Jill prelim", "Demo Classic J&J prelim"

2. **Select or Replace Your Judge Name**
   - A list of judges assigned to this competition will appear
   - Find and tap your name
   - **If replacing another judge**: Tap the name of the judge you're replacing (e.g., if Carl is unable to continue, you would tap "Carl")

3. **Modify Name if Needed**
   - After selecting a name, you'll see a confirmation screen showing the selected judge name
   - If there's a spelling error or if you're replacing someone, you can modify the name here
   - Tap the name field to edit
   - Enter the correct name

4. **Start the Round**
   - After confirming your name is correct, tap the **START** button
   - The system will load your judging interface
   - You're now ready to begin scoring

**Important Notes:**
- If you select the wrong competition, you can navigate back and select the correct one
- Name changes made during login are saved in the system
- Your judge ID (e.g., "L3054") is assigned by the system for tracking and replay purposes

### 2.3 Understanding the Main Screen

Once you've started a round, you'll see the main judging interface. Understanding this screen is essential for efficient judging.

**Header Section (Top of Screen):**

Located at the very top, this section displays:
- **Event Name**: "Demoevent" or the competition name
- **Competition/Round**: e.g., "Demo Jack&Jill prelim - Leader"
- **Judge Name**: Your name and Judge ID (e.g., "Peter Parker (Judge L3054)")
- **Competition Stats**: 
  - "Couples: 15" - Total number of couples in this round
  - "Yes: 5" - Number of couples to advance (the "cut")
  - "Alt: 3" - Number of alternate positions available
- **Time**: Current time display (e.g., "Sun 17:05")

**Heat Tabs (Below Header):**

Competitions may be divided into multiple heats when there are many competitors.

- **heat 1**: First group of dancers
- **heat 2**: Second group of dancers (if applicable)
- **all**: View all dancers across all heats combined

You can tap these tabs to switch between views. However, you must judge ALL heats before completing the round.

**Main Scoring Area (Center):**

This is where you'll see the list of competitors with their:
- **BIB Number**: Large number on the left (e.g., "86", "124", "136")
- **Competitor Name**: Name of the dancer or couple
- **Role Indicator**: "L" for Leader or "F" for Follower (appears as a badge)
- **Score/Status Indicators**: Visual representation of your scores
- **Color Coding**: 
  - Green = Yes/High scores
  - Yellow = Alternate/Medium scores
  - Red = No/Low scores
  - Pink/Salmon = Low scores in prelims

Each competitor row shows their current score as a horizontal bar graph, with numerical values displayed.

**Bottom Navigation Tabs:**

Four tabs for different viewing modes:
- **ALL**: Shows all competitors in order
- **RANKED**: Shows competitors sorted by score (not recommended for judging)
- **TODO**: Shows only competitors you haven't scored yet
- **CONFLICTS**: Shows any scoring conflicts (primarily in RAW and TheOpen modes)

**Top Right Icons:**

- **Music Note Icon**: Audio controls (if applicable)
- **Eye Icon**: Screen blanking feature for privacy
- **Settings Icon (Gear)**: Access judging mode and display options
- **Done Button**: Shows percentage complete and allows final submission

## 3. Judging Interface Overview

### 3.1 Competition Information Display

The header provides crucial information about what you're judging and your assignment.

**Competition Type and Division:**
- Clearly identifies the event: "Demo Jack&Jill prelim", "Demo Jack&Jill final", etc.
- For Jack & Jill competitions, indicates the skill level (Novice, Intermediate, Advanced, All Star, Champions)
- For age-based divisions, shows Juniors, Sophisticated, or Masters
- For other formats, shows Classic, Showcase, Rising Star, etc.

**Your Assigned Role:**
The system indicates whether you're judging:
- **Leaders (L)**: You score only the Leader role
- **Followers (F)**: You score only the Follower role
- **Both Roles**: In some competitions with fewer dancers, you may be assigned to score both

This is indicated by the badge next to competitor names. If you see "(L)" next to your name in the header, you're judging Leaders and should focus only on the Leader in each partnership.

**Couples and Cuts Information:**
- **Couples**: Total number of partnerships competing
- **Yes**: Number of couples that should advance to the next round
- **Alt**: Number of alternate positions (these are "bubble" couples that may advance if someone scratches)

**Judge ID Reference:**
Your unique Judge ID (e.g., "L3054") serves several purposes:
- Internal tracking within the scoring system
- Allows for score review and verification
- Enables score replay if technical issues occur
- Maintains audit trail for WSDC compliance

### 3.2 Icons and Controls

**Eye Icon (Screen Blanking):**

Located in the top right corner, the eye icon provides privacy protection.

- **Purpose**: Prevents others from seeing your scores
- **How it works**: Tap the icon and your screen dims/grays out completely
- **When to use**: 
  - During finals when you don't want competitors or spectators to see your scores
  - When discussing the competition with other judges
  - During breaks when you've set the tablet down
- **How to restore**: Simply tap anywhere on the screen to make scores visible again
- **Note**: You can still score while the screen is blanked; the system continues to function normally

**Settings Icon (Gear):**

Opens a menu with various options:
- Judging mode selection (Scale, RAW, TheOpen)
- Display preferences (competitor names, marshalling status)
- Refresh/Reload function
- Additional customization options

**Done Button:**

The Done button is your final submission control:
- Shows a percentage (e.g., "85%", "100%") indicating how many competitors you've scored
- Turns from gray to filled green when 100% complete
- Contains the text "done" when ready for submission
- Shows "OFFLINE" in red if network connection is lost
- Becomes unclickable (red) when offline

**Time Display:**

Shows current time and day (e.g., "Sun 17:05"):
- Helps you track round duration
- Ensures you're aware of time constraints
- Updates continuously

**Additional Controls:**

Depending on the judging mode and competition format, you may see:
- Plus (+) and Minus (-) buttons for fine-tuning scores
- Numbered buttons (0-9) for direct score entry
- Color and icon selectors for marking competitors
- Emoji buttons for adding notes

## 4. Judging Modes Explained

The judging tablet system offers multiple scoring modes to accommodate different judging preferences and styles. All modes can be used in both preliminary rounds and finals - the system adapts the display to show either callbacks (Yes/No/Alt) or placements accordingly.

### 4.1 Scale Mode (Default/Recommended)

Scale Mode is the most intuitive and widely used judging method. It uses a visual slider approach where you double-click on a horizontal scale to indicate your evaluation.

**How It Works:**

The scale is divided into five zones, each representing approximately 20% of the total range:

1. **Far Left (0-20%)**: Absolutely No / Definite No
2. **Left-Center (20-40%)**: No
3. **Center (40-60%)**: Maybe / Neutral
4. **Right-Center (60-80%)**: Yes
5. **Far Right (80-100%)**: Absolutely Yes / Definite Yes

**Scoring Process:**

1. Watch a competitor dance
2. Double-click on the scale where your evaluation falls
   - The further right you click, the better the score
   - The further left you click, the lower the score
3. The system measures the exact position of your click
4. A raw score is calculated with precision to seven decimal places (e.g., 67.3456789)
5. Visual feedback appears immediately:
   - A colored bar shows the score
   - A small number displays the raw score
   - The color indicates the evaluation level (green = high, yellow = medium, red = low)

**In Preliminary Rounds:**

Your raw score is automatically converted to the WSDC callback system:
- High scores → **Yes** (displayed in green)
- Medium-high scores → **Alt1** (lightest yellow)
- Medium scores → **Alt2** (medium yellow)
- Medium-low scores → **Alt3** (darker yellow)
- Low scores → **No** (displayed in red or pink)

The exact thresholds are managed by the Head Judge and the system configuration.

**In Finals:**

Your raw score directly contributes to the competitor's final placement:
- Highest score receives 1st place
- Second highest receives 2nd place
- And so on...

**Visual Feedback:**

In the main scoring area, you'll see:
- A horizontal bar representing the score range
- The bar fills from left to right based on your score
- A number displayed within or near the bar showing the raw score (e.g., "67.3")
- Color coding to quickly identify score levels

**Advantages of Scale Mode:**

- Intuitive and fast
- Prevents exact duplicate scores (due to 7-decimal precision)
- Visual feedback helps you remember previous scores
- Easy to adjust scores by clicking in a different position
- Recommended for most judges and competitions

**Correcting Scores:**

If you mis-score a competitor:
1. Simply double-click again in the correct position on the scale
2. The system immediately updates to the new raw score
3. Previous score is overwritten

### 4.2 RAW Mode

RAW Mode allows you to manually enter specific numerical scores using a numeric keypad interface.

**How It Works:**

1. You see a numeric button interface (0-9)
2. You tap three digits to enter a score
3. The score is entered in the format: XX.X
   - First two taps = whole number
   - Third tap = decimal place
4. Example: Tapping 7, then 5, then 3 enters a score of **75.3**

**When to Use RAW Mode:**

- When you prefer precise numerical control
- When you have a specific scoring system in mind
- When you want to enter exact predetermined values
- For experienced judges who think in numerical terms

**Scoring Range:**

- Typical range: 0.0 to 100.0
- You can enter any value within the system's accepted range
- The system may have configured minimum and maximum values

**Visual Feedback:**

- The entered number appears on screen
- Color coding applies based on the value (green for high, yellow for medium, red for low)
- The numerical value is stored as your raw score

**Correcting Scores:**

To change a score:
1. Tap the competitor again
2. Enter three new digits
3. The system overwrites the previous score

**Important Notes:**

- RAW mode can result in score conflicts (two competitors with identical scores)
- The CONFLICTS tab will alert you if duplicate scores exist
- You should resolve conflicts before submitting (DONE)
- Conflicts prevent the DONE button from being available

### 4.3 TheOpen Mode

TheOpen Mode combines elements of both Scale and RAW modes, offering a range-based scoring system with fine-tuning controls.

**How It Works:**

You have multiple input methods:

**Method 1: Range Selection (70-100 and "No")**
- Buttons display values from 70 to 98 (even numbers: 70, 72, 74, 76, 78, 80, 82, 84, 86, 88, 90, 92, 94, 96, 98)
- A "No" button for elimination
- Tap a button to assign that base score
- After selecting a base, use plus/minus for fine-tuning

**Method 2: Numeric Keypad (0-9)**
- Similar to RAW mode
- Tap three digits to enter a specific score
- Format: First digit, Second digit, Third digit creates XX.X
- Example: 7, 5, 3 = 75.3

**Method 3: Plus/Minus Fine-Tuning**
- After selecting a base score, use:
  - **Plus (+) button**: Increase score by small increments (typically 0.1 or 1.0)
  - **Minus (-) button**: Decrease score by small increments
- Allows precise adjustments without re-entering entire score

**Display:**

- Current score shows prominently
- Plus and minus buttons appear next to score
- Color coding applies (green/yellow/red)
- Placement number appears on the right (in finals)

**When to Use TheOpen Mode:**

- When you want the precision of numbers with easier input than RAW
- When you prefer working within a defined range
- When you like the ability to fine-tune after initial scoring
- For competitions using a specific scoring range (e.g., 70-100 scale)

**Correcting Scores:**

Multiple options:
1. Tap a different range button
2. Use plus/minus to adjust
3. Enter new digits via numeric keypad
4. Latest input becomes the final score

**Important Notes:**

- Like RAW mode, TheOpen can create score conflicts
- Check the CONFLICTS tab before submitting
- The range (70-100) is commonly used but may vary by system configuration

### 4.4 Scale (2 songs) Mode

This specialized mode is designed for spotlight finals where each couple dances to two separate songs.

**Purpose:**

In some finals formats, particularly Jack & Jill spotlight finals, couples dance twice:
- First song (often slower or different genre)
- Second song (often faster or contrasting style)

This mode allows judges to score each song separately, then automatically averages the two scores.

**How It Works:**

**During First Song:**
1. The system indicates "Song 1" or "First Dance"
2. Score each couple using your preferred method (Scale, RAW, or TheOpen)
3. Scores are saved but not finalized

**During Second Song:**
2. The system switches to "Song 2" or "Second Dance"
3. Score each couple again for their second performance
4. You can score completely differently based on the second song

**Calculation:**
- System automatically calculates the average of both scores
- Example: Song 1 = 80.0, Song 2 = 90.0, Final Score = 85.0
- The average score determines final placement

**Visual Indication:**

- Clear labeling shows which song you're currently scoring
- You may see both scores displayed side by side
- The averaged final score appears after both songs are complete

**Benefits:**

- Allows evaluation of different aspects of dancing (musicality to different tempos, style versatility)
- Reduces impact of one poor performance
- Provides more comprehensive assessment
- Prevents judges from trying to mentally average - system does it automatically

**When This Mode is Used:**

- Spotlight finals with two-song format
- Some Classic Jack & Jill finals
- Invitational competitions with multiple song requirements
- Any competition where organizers want comprehensive evaluation

**Important Notes:**

- Both songs must be scored for the average to calculate
- You can use different judging modes (Scale/RAW/TheOpen) for each song
- The system tracks which song is playing
- Head Judge controls when the system switches from song 1 to song 2

## 5. Preliminary Rounds (Callbacks)

Preliminary rounds use the WSDC Callback System to determine which competitors advance to the next round or to finals. Understanding this system is crucial for effective judging.

### 5.1 Understanding Callback Scoring

**WSDC Callback System:**

The World Swing Dance Council has standardized the callback system with specific point values:

| Callback | Point Value | Meaning | Color Display |
|----------|-------------|---------|---------------|
| Yes | 10 | Definitely advance | Green |
| Alt 1 | 4.5 | Strong alternate | Light Yellow |
| Alt 2 | 4.3 | Medium alternate | Medium Yellow |
| Alt 3 | 4.2 | Bubble alternate | Darker Yellow |
| No | 0 | Do not advance | Red/Pink |

**How Callbacks Work:**

1. Each judge scores competitors as Yes, Alt (1-3), or No
2. The system sums all judges' point values for each competitor
3. Competitors are ranked by total points
4. The top scorers (based on the "cut" number) advance to the next round

**The "Cut":**

The competition header shows:
- **Yes: 5** means the top 5 couples should advance
- **Alt: 3** means 3 additional alternate positions are available

Alternates may advance if:
- Someone scratches (withdraws)
- The competition structure allows for more dancers
- The Head Judge determines additional callbacks are appropriate

**Raw Scores in Preliminaries:**

While you see Yes/No/Alt categories, the system works with raw scores underneath:

- When you score in Scale Mode, your double-click position generates a raw score
- The system converts this raw score to a callback category
- Example raw score ranges (configured by Head Judge):
  - 80-100 → Yes
  - 60-79 → Alt 1
  - 50-59 → Alt 2
  - 40-49 → Alt 3
  - 0-39 → No

**Why Raw Scores Matter:**

The Head Judge must assign unique numerical raw scores to all competitors (WSDC requirement). Your raw score:
- Breaks ties within callback categories
- Provides granular ranking
- Enables Head Judge to adjust cuts if needed
- Maintains data for analytics and review

The seven-decimal precision (e.g., 67.3456789) makes duplicate scores virtually impossible in Scale Mode.

### 5.2 Judging Workflow in Preliminaries

**Timing Your Judging:**

You have flexibility in when you score:

**Option 1: Score While Dancing (Recommended for Large Competitions)**
- Watch competitors dance
- Score each couple/dancer as they pass by
- Update scores as you continue watching
- Most efficient for large heats

**Option 2: Score After Song Ends**
- Watch entire song
- Mental notes during dancing
- Score all competitors after music stops
- Better for smaller heats or if you prefer comprehensive observation

**Option 3: Hybrid Approach**
- Initial scores during dancing
- Adjustments after song ends
- Balances efficiency with thorough evaluation

**Using Heats Effectively:**

When a competition has many competitors, they're divided into heats.

**Example: 44 Couples Total**
- Heat 1: 22 couples (BIBs 1-22)
- Heat 2: 22 couples (BIBs 23-44)

**Important Rules About Heats:**

1. You must judge ALL heats before the DONE button becomes available
2. Each heat dances to a separate song
3. All heats are judged using the same criteria
4. Scores are combined across all heats to determine who advances
5. The "all" tab shows combined results

**Workflow for Multiple Heats:**

1. Heat 1 plays → Score all dancers in Heat 1
2. Heat 2 plays → Score all dancers in Heat 2
3. Switch to "all" tab → Review overall scoring balance
4. Make adjustments if needed across heats
5. Submit when 100% complete

**Navigating Between Heats:**

- Tap "heat 1" tab to see only first heat dancers
- Tap "heat 2" tab to see only second heat dancers
- Tap "all" tab to see everyone combined
- Current heat is highlighted

**Best Practices for Heat Judging:**

- Score consistently across heats (don't judge second heat harder or easier)
- Use markers to identify standout dancers in each heat
- Review "all" view to ensure appropriate distribution of Yes/No/Alt scores
- Don't compare dancers between heats - judge each heat independently, then review overall

### 5.3 Viewing Options

The four tabs at the bottom of the screen provide different ways to view and manage your scoring.

**ALL Tab (Default View)**

**What It Shows:**
- All competitors in the order they appear on the floor
- Typically sorted by BIB number
- Shows full list regardless of scoring status

**When to Use:**
- **Primary judging mode** - recommended for most judging
- When you want to see everyone in predictable order
- To get a complete overview of the competition
- To find specific BIB numbers easily

**Advantages:**
- Predictable, stable layout
- Easy to scan through all competitors
- BIB numbers in logical sequence
- No distracting resort as you score

**Best Practice:** **Do your primary judging in the ALL tab.**

---

**RANKED Tab**

**What It Shows:**
- All competitors sorted by their current raw scores
- Highest scores at top, lowest at bottom
- Dynamically re-sorts as you score

**When to Use:**
- Quick overview of current standings after initial scoring
- Final review before submitting
- Checking overall score distribution

**When NOT to Use:**
- **Do NOT use RANKED tab for primary judging**
- The constant re-sorting is confusing
- Dancers move position as you score them
- Easy to lose track of who you've scored

**Why It Can Be Confusing:**

Example scenario:
1. You score Dancer A with 85.0 (they move to top of list)
2. You score Dancer B with 90.0 (they jump above Dancer A)
3. Dancer A drops down in the list
4. You might score Dancer A again, thinking you haven't scored them
5. Layout constantly shifts, making it difficult to work systematically

**Valid Uses:**
- After completing all scoring in ALL tab
- To verify your Yes scores are appropriate
- To check score distribution and ensure adequate spread
- For final quality check

---

**TODO Tab**

**What It Shows:**
- Only competitors you haven't scored yet
- Empty/null scores
- Unscored dancers highlighted

**When to Use:**
- In large competitions when you're partially complete
- To identify which dancers still need scoring
- When working through a heat systematically
- Final check to ensure no one was missed

**Benefits:**
- Focuses attention on remaining work
- Reduces clutter in large competitions
- Prevents missing dancers
- Helps track progress

**Workflow Example:**

In a large heat with 30 dancers:
1. Start in ALL tab, score most dancers
2. At 85% complete, switch to TODO tab
3. See only the 4-5 remaining unscored dancers
4. Score them
5. TODO tab becomes empty
6. Switch back to ALL for final review

**Progress Tracking:**

The TODO count correlates with your percentage complete:
- 30 in TODO, 0% done
- 15 in TODO, 50% done
- 5 in TODO, 83% done
- 0 in TODO, 100% done

---

**CONFLICTS Tab**

**What It Shows:**
- Competitors who have identical raw scores
- Duplicate values that need resolution
- Only populated when using RAW or TheOpen modes

**When Conflicts Occur:**

Conflicts happen when two or more competitors receive exactly the same score:
- In RAW mode: You enter 75.3 for two different dancers
- In TheOpen mode: You assign 84 to multiple dancers

**Why Conflicts Matter:**

- WSDC system requires unique raw scores for all competitors
- Tied raw scores create ambiguity in ranking
- May prevent proper callback calculation
- System cannot determine advancement order

**In Scale Mode:**

Conflicts are virtually impossible due to seven-decimal precision:
- Your click position: 67.234567% = score of 67.234567
- Next person's click: 67.234892% = score of 67.234892
- These are different scores, even if displayed as "67.2"

**Resolving Conflicts:**

When the CONFLICTS tab shows entries:

1. Review the conflicting dancers
2. Reassess their dancing
3. Assign different scores (in RAW or TheOpen mode)
4. Even a 0.1 point difference resolves the conflict
5. Continue until CONFLICTS tab is empty

**System Behavior:**

- Unresolved conflicts may prevent submission
- DONE button may remain unavailable until conflicts are cleared
- Some systems allow Head Judge to break ties with their scores

**Best Practice:** Use Scale Mode to avoid conflicts entirely.

## 6. Finals Rounds (Placements)

Finals use a placement-based scoring system where judges rank competitors from 1st place through the final placement. The system uses Relative Placement scoring according to WSDC standards.

### 6.1 Understanding Finals Scoring

**How Placement Scoring Works:**

Unlike preliminaries where you give Yes/No/Alt callbacks, in finals you're creating a ranked order:

1. You score each competitor (using Scale, RAW, or TheOpen mode)
2. The system sorts all competitors by their raw scores
3. **Highest raw score = 1st place**
4. **Second highest = 2nd place**
5. **Third highest = 3rd place**
6. And so on through all finalists

**Visual Display in Finals:**

- You'll see large placement numbers on the right side of each row
- Example: "1", "2", "3", "4", "5", "6"
- These update automatically as you score
- The placement corresponds to the raw score ranking

**Example:**

| Dancer | Your Raw Score | Automatic Placement |
|--------|---------------|---------------------|
| BIB 555 | 96.0 | 1 |
| BIB 123 | 90.0 | 3 |
| BIB 93 | 70.7 | 5 |
| BIB 175 | 94.0 | 2 |
| BIB 124 | 70.0 | 6 |
| BIB 179 | 84.0 | 4 |

**Relative Placement System:**

The overall competition results are calculated using Relative Placement:
- All judges' placements are combined
- Lower total placement numbers = better overall placement
- System follows WSDC Relative Placement rules
- Tied placements are broken by majority rule

**Point Awards:**

WSDC points are awarded based on:
- Final placement (1st, 2nd, 3rd, etc.)
- Competition tier (based on number of competitors)
- Higher tiers award more points

(See Appendix for detailed point charts)

### 6.2 Judging Finals

**Competition Formats:**

Finals can be structured in different ways:

**Spotlight Finals:**
- Couples dance one at a time
- Each couple gets dedicated music and floor time
- Typical duration: 1-2 minutes per couple
- Allows detailed observation of each partnership
- Common in Champions and invitational divisions

**All-Skate Finals:**
- All finalists dance simultaneously
- Similar to preliminary rounds but fewer dancers
- Must track multiple couples at once
- More common in larger divisions
- Typically 1-2 songs

**Classic Jack & Jill Finals with Heats:**
- Finalists may be divided into heats
- Each heat dances separately
- Scores are combined across heats
- Less common, but follows same heat judging principles as preliminaries

**Timing Your Scoring:**

**For Spotlight Finals:**

Most judges score **after each couple finishes**:
1. Watch the couple dance their full song
2. Immediately score when they finish
3. System moves to next couple
4. Clear mental reset between couples

Some judges score **during the performance**:
- Enter preliminary score while watching
- Adjust as performance continues
- Finalize when song ends

**For All-Skate Finals:**

**During the song:**
- Watch all couples
- Mental notes on standouts
- May enter scores as you watch

**After the song:**
- Review and finalize all scores
- Adjust based on overall comparison
- Ensure appropriate spread of placements

**Judges decide their own workflow** - the system supports both approaches.

**Classic Jack & Jill Format:**

Classic Jack & Jill contests have unique characteristics:
- Partners are assigned for entire final (not rotating)
- May include multiple songs or heats
- Both partners' skills contribute to overall presentation
- Judge the partnership as a unit

### 6.3 Reading Finals Results

In finals view, each competitor row shows:

**Left Side:**
- BIB number (large)
- Competitor name(s)
- Role indicator if applicable

**Center:**

**Right Side:**
- **Large placement number** (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, etc.)

- Updates automatically as you score

**Color Coding in Finals:**

Depending on system configuration:
- Light blue = higher placements
- Darker blue/gray = lower placements
- Color intensity may correlate with score

**Minimum Finalists:**

**WSDC Requirement:**
- WSDC Jack and Jill competitions must have **minimum 5 unique Leaders and 5 unique Followers** in finals for points to be awarded
- This is per role, not per couple
- Head Judge ensures this minimum is met

**Typical Finals Sizes:**
- Smaller divisions: 6-8 finalists
- Mid-size divisions: 10-12 finalists

**Multiple Judges:**

Finals require odd numbers of judges (WSDC standard):
- Minimum: 5 judges

**Your Role:**

- Final results are calculated by the system using Relative Placement

**After Scoring:**

## 9. Completing Your Judging

Properly finishing and submitting your scores is crucial for competition integrity and smooth operations.

### 9.1 Monitoring Your Progress

**Percentage Indicator:**

The DONE button contains a percentage that shows your completion status.

**How It's Calculated:**

```
Percentage = (Number of Scored Competitors / Total Active Competitors) × 100%
```

**Examples:**

- 30 dancers, 0 scored = **0%**
- 30 dancers, 15 scored = **50%**
- 30 dancers, 27 scored = **90%**
- 30 dancers, 30 scored = **100%**

**Visual Changes:**

As you progress:
- **0-99%**: DONE button shows percentage in gray
- **100%**: DONE button fills with green color
- **100%**: Button becomes clickable
- **< 100%**: Button remains unclickable

**What Counts Toward Completion:**

- Each active (non-scratched) competitor must have a score
- Scratched competitors are automatically excluded
- All heats must be completed
- Every dancer needs a raw score assigned

**What Doesn't Affect Completion:**

- Markers and notes (optional)
- Viewing styles or settings
- Which tab you're viewing
- Order you scored dancers

**Using Percentage to Guide Your Work:**

- **0-25%**: Just getting started
- **25-50%**: Making good progress
- **50-75%**: Over halfway done
- **75-90%**: Nearly complete, check TODO tab
- **90-99%**: Very close, a few remain
- **100%**: Ready to submit!

**Finding Missing Scores:**

If you're at 95% and can't find who you missed:

1. Switch to **TODO tab**
2. See exactly who needs scoring
3. Score remaining dancers
4. Return to 100%

### 9.2 The DONE Button

The DONE button is your final submission control and digital signature.

**Button States:**

**1. Inactive (Gray with Percentage < 100%)**
- Button shows current percentage
- Cannot be clicked
- Keep scoring until 100%

**2. Ready (Green with "done" and 100%)**
- Button filled with green color
- Shows "done" text
- Clickable and ready for submission
- All competitors have been scored

**3. Offline (Red with "OFFLINE")**
- Red background
- Text says "OFFLINE"
- Network connection lost
- Cannot submit until connection restored

**Submission Process:**

When you reach 100% and the button is green:

**Step 1: Click DONE**
- Tap the green DONE button
- System prepares your submission

**Step 2: Confirmation Prompt**
- Dialog box appears asking: **"Are you absolutely sure?"**
- This is your final verification
- Once confirmed, you cannot change scores

**Step 3: Confirm**
- Tap "OK" or "Yes" to confirm
- Tap "Cancel" or "No" to return to judging

**Step 4: Submission**
- System sends your complete scoring data to server
- All raw scores, placements, and metadata transmitted
- Timestamp recorded

**Step 5: Confirmation**
- **Large green checkmark** (✓) appears
- Submission successful
- Your judging is complete for this round

**What the Green Checkmark Means:**

- ✓ Your scores have been received by the system
- ✓ Your judging is finalized
- ✓ You can no longer modify scores on this tablet
- ✓ This serves as your digital signature
- ✓ You're confirming accuracy to the best of your knowledge

**Finality of Submission:**

Once you see the green checkmark:

- **Scores are locked** on your tablet
- You cannot make changes
- System uses your scores for calculations
- Only Head Judge/Scorer can reopen (in exceptional circumstances)

**Why "Absolutely Sure" Prompt Exists:**

This double confirmation prevents:
- Accidental submissions
- Premature completion
- Unintended score locking
- Last-second panic clicks

**Take your time** - review your scores before clicking DONE, and carefully consider before confirming.

**After Submission:**

1. The round is complete for you
2. Return your tablet to the Judge Meeting Point
3. Wait for other judges to complete
4. System calculates final results when all judges submit
5. Results are posted once calculations complete

**What If You Made a Mistake?**

If you realize an error AFTER clicking DONE:

1. **Immediately notify the Head Judge**
2. Explain the specific issue
3. Head Judge can reopen your tablet (exceptional cases only)
4. Make the correction
5. Re-submit

**This should be rare** - double check before final submission.

**Issue: I scored the wrong dancer**

**Solution:**
- Simply score them again with the correct value

**Solution:**

  - Click "Cancel" or "No"

  - **Immediately find the Head Judge**
  - Explain the situation
  - Head Judge can reopen your tablet (exceptional cases only)
  - Complete your scoring
  - Re-submit properly

**Prevention:**
- Always check percentage is 100% before clicking DONE
- Review TODO tab before submitting
- Take a breath before confirming

---

**Issue: I have duplicate scores (Conflicts)**

**This only happens in RAW or TheOpen modes**

**Solution:**
1. Tap the **CONFLICTS tab**
2. See which dancers have identical scores
3. Select one of the conflicting dancers
4. Change their score slightly
   - Even 0.1 difference resolves conflict
   - Example: Change 75.0 to 75.1
5. Repeat until CONFLICTS tab is empty
6. Then you can submit

**Prevention:**
- Use Scale mode (conflicts are virtually impossible)
- In RAW/TheOpen, deliberately vary scores

---

**Issue: The percentage won't reach 100%**

**Common Causes:**

1. **One or more dancers not scored**
   - Check TODO tab
   - Score all remaining dancers

2. **Multiple heats not complete**
   - Switch to each heat tab
   - Verify all dancers in each heat are scored
   - Check "all" tab

3. **Scratched dancer confusion**
   - Scratched dancers (strikethrough) don't need scores
   - They're automatically excluded

**Solution:**
- Use TODO tab to find missing scores
- Review all heat tabs systematically
- Score everyone without strikethrough

---

**Issue: I can't find a specific BIB number**

4. **Look systematically through the list**

---

**Issue: My screen went blank/gray**

**Solution:**
- You likely activated the eye icon (screen blanking)

**Issue: I marked someone with the wrong color/icon**

**Issue: Tablet is running very slowly**

**Issue: I lost my place in a large all-skate**

**3. Avoid RANKED Tab for Active Judging**
- Constant re-sorting is confusing
- Easy to score dancers twice
- Lose track of position
- Use only for post-scoring review

**4. Judge Independently**
- Don't look at other judges' tablets
- Make your own evaluations
- System integrity depends on independent judging
- Comparing scores during judging is inappropriate

**5. Double-Check Before DONE**
- Review TODO tab one final time
- Scan through scores in ALL tab
- Verify percentage is 100%
- Take 10 seconds to review
- Then submit with confidence

## 11. Head Judge Specific Information

This section is primarily for Head Judges and officials managing the judging process.

### 11.1 Head Judge Responsibilities

**Tablet Distribution:**

Before each round:

1. **Verify tablet count**
   - Confirm all tablets present and charged
   - Test each tablet briefly
   - Ensure network connectivity

2. **Assign tablets to judges**
   - Track which judge has which tablet
   - Record assignments for reference
   - Consider judge preferences (mode settings)

3. **Brief judges**
   - Explain round format
   - Clarify any special instructions
   - Answer questions

**During Competition:**

1. **Monitor progress**
   - Check if judges are having difficulties
   - Watch for raised hands or signals
   - Be available for questions

2. **Make announcements**
   - When to refresh
   - Competition changes
   - Time updates

3. **Handle technical issues**
   - Tablet malfunctions
   - Connectivity problems
   - Score disputes

**After Round:**

1. **Collect tablets**
   - Verify all judges submitted (green checkmarks)
   - Check for any unsubmitted scores
   - Collect tablets at Judge Meeting Point

2. **Verify completion**
   - All judges at 100%
   - No missing scores
   - Results ready for calculation

### 11.2 Managing Judges

**Emergency Tablet Replacement:**

When a judge needs a new tablet:

1. Verify the issue (malfunction, battery, damage)
2. Record which tablet is being replaced
3. If possible, recover scores from old tablet
4. Assign new tablet
5. Judge logs in again
6. May need to re-score if data not recoverable

**Reopening Locked Tablets:**

**Only in exceptional circumstances:**

When a judge accidentally submits prematurely:

1. Verify the legitimate need
2. Access administrative controls
3. Reopen the specific tablet
4. Allow judge to complete/correct scoring
5. Judge re-submits when truly complete
6. Document the incident

**This should be rare** - judges should be careful with final submission.

**Handling Scoring Discrepancies:**

If scores seem incorrect or anomalous:

1. Review with the judge privately
2. Verify intentional vs. accidental scores
3. Check for technical errors
4. If genuine error, may allow correction
5. If intentional judgment, scores stand
6. Document any interventions

**Raw Score Requirements (WSDC):**

- Chief Judge (or Secondary Chief Judge/Raw Score Judge) must assign unique raw scores
- Even in callback system, raw scores differentiate ties
- System should prevent duplicate raw scores
- Review conflicts before finalizing results

**Managing Conflicts:**

- Monitor CONFLICTS tabs across judges
- Ensure resolution before calculations
- Provide guidance on breaking ties
- Use Chief Judge raw scores as tiebreakers if needed

### 12.3 Glossary of Terms

**ALL Tab**: View showing all competitors in numerical/entry order

**Alt / Alternate**: Callback category between Yes and No; dancers who may advance if spots open

**BIB Number**: Large number identifying each competitor

**Callback**: Preliminary round scoring system indicating who advances

**Chief Judge**: Head official responsible for competition oversight and integrity

**Conflicts**: When two or more competitors have identical raw scores

**DONE Button**: Final submission control; becomes green at 100% completion

**Heat**: Subset of competitors when division is split into groups

**Head Judge**: Same as Chief Judge in some contexts; senior judging official

**Marshalling**: Check-in process confirming competitor attendance

**Placement**: Final ranking (1st, 2nd, 3rd, etc.) in finals

**RAW Mode**: Judging mode using manual numeric entry

**Raw Score**: Underlying numerical value assigned to each competitor

**RANKED Tab**: View sorting competitors by current scores (not recommended for judging)

**Relative Placement**: WSDC-standard system for calculating final results from multiple judges' placements

**Scale Mode**: Default judging mode using double-click on horizontal spectrum

**Scratched**: Competitor who withdrew or didn't appear; indicated by strikethrough

**Spotlight**: Finals format where couples dance individually

**TheOpen Mode**: Judging mode using 70-100 range with fine-tuning controls

**TODO Tab**: View showing only competitors not yet scored

**WSDC**: World Swing Dance Council - governing body for West Coast Swing competitions

### 12.4 Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What if I don't know the competitors - should I turn on "Show Competitor Names"?**

A: Personal preference. Many judges keep names off to reduce bias and judge purely on dancing. Enable names if it helps you track dancers in spotlight finals.

---

**Q: Can I switch judging modes in the middle of a round?**

A: Yes. Your previous scores remain; you just change how you'll score going forward. However, it's best to stick with one mode for consistency.

---

**Q: What happens if I score someone twice by accident?**

A: The latest score overwrites the previous score. No harm done - just make sure the final score reflects your true evaluation.

---

**Q: Do I have to score during the dance, or can I wait until after?**

A: Your choice. Many judges score continuously during all-skates and after each dance in spotlights, but you can develop your own workflow.

---

**Q: What if I'm not finished when the music stops?**

A: No problem. Continue scoring based on your observations. There's no requirement to finish during the music.

---

**Q: Can other judges see my scores?**

A: No. Each tablet is private. Other judges cannot see your scores, markers, or notes.

---

**Q: What if I disagree with another judge's scores?**

A: That's normal and expected. Each judge evaluates independently. The system combines all judges using Relative Placement to determine fair results.

---

**Q: How do I know if my tablet is working correctly?**

A: The system provides real-time feedback. If you see scores appearing when you tap/click, and the percentage updates as you score, it's working. If anything seems frozen or unresponsive, notify Head Judge immediately.

---

**Q: What's the difference between Alt 1, Alt 2, and Alt 3?**

A: They represent levels of "maybe" between definite yes and definite no:
- Alt 1: Strong maybe, close to yes
- Alt 2: Medium maybe, truly uncertain
- Alt 3: Weak maybe, closer to no

The point values (4.5, 4.3, 4.2) help rank them.

---

**Q: Why can't I use an even number like 4 or 6 judges in finals?**

A: WSDC requires odd numbers to prevent tied votes in Relative Placement calculations. 5 or 7 judges ensures clear majority for each placement.

---

**Q: What if there's a tie in my scores?**

A: In Scale Mode, ties are virtually impossible (7-decimal precision). In RAW or TheOpen modes, the CONFLICTS tab will show ties, and you should adjust scores slightly to differentiate.

---

**Q: Can I change my scores after clicking DONE?**

A: Only in exceptional circumstances with Head Judge approval. The DONE button is a final submission - review carefully before confirming.

---

**Q: What happens to my markers and notes?**

A: They're personal to your judging experience and not included in official records. They help you track dancers but don't affect scoring or results.

---

**Q: Do I need to score scratched dancers?**

A: No. Scratched dancers (strikethrough names) are automatically excluded. The system doesn't count them toward your completion percentage.

---

**Q: What's the Judge ID number for?**

A: Internal system tracking, allows score review, and enables replay if needed. It's for audit trails and technical purposes.

---

**Q: How long does battery last?**

A: Tablets should be fully charged at start. Most last 4-6 hours. If you see low battery warnings, notify officials immediately.

---

**Q: What if I need to leave mid-round?**

A: Notify Head Judge immediately. They'll handle the situation - possibly another judge replaces you or your partial scores are noted. Don't just leave.

---

**Q: Why do some competitions use different point ranges (like 70-100)?**

A: Different organizations have different traditions. TheOpen Mode accommodates these. The important thing is judges in the same competition use the same system.

---

**Q: Is there a time limit for judging each round?**

A: Generally no hard limit, but be respectful of competition schedule. Work efficiently but thoroughly. Most rounds complete within reasonable time.

---

**Q: What if I think there was a scoring error in the final results?**

A: Speak with the Head Judge privately after results are posted. They can review scoring data. Don't publicly dispute results.

---

**Q: Can I practice on the tablet before the competition?**

A: Ask Head Judge or officials. Some events offer brief practice/orientation. Familiarity with the system helps you judge more effectively.

---

**Q: What's the most important thing to remember?**

A: **Judge fairly, independently, and to the best of your ability.** The system is a tool - your expertise and integrity are what truly matter.
